# cerebral-oximetry-phantom
Title: Cerebral oximetry phantom module

Contains STL files for 3D printing of cerebrovascular modules - the main component for a published cerebral oximetry test method

Keywords: Phantom, Cerebral oximeter, NIRS, brain, Blood oxygen saturation

Description:
These cerebrovascular modules (CVM) were developed by Dr. Joshua Pfefer’s group in FDA as part of a phantom-based test method for evaluating of cerebral oximetry performance accuracy 

CVMs were fabricated using a stereolithography 3D‐printer (Form2, Formlabs, Inc., Somerville, MA, USA) based on UV-curing of customized methacrylate photopolymer resin composites well‐matched with human brain optical properties.  The CVMs were constructed with overall dimensions of 120 x 120 x 50 mm, with the perfused channel-array region limited to a volume of 120 x 80 x 32.5 mm with channel length of 106 mm in two forms (high and low ctHb levels by modifying the channel diameter) and adult/neonate by customizing the ratio of resins to represent optical preparties for the specified age range. CVMs incorporate a total of 119 cylindrical linear channels spaced 4.36 mm apart in the vertical and 4.58 – 4.6 mm horizontal directions, with the top row of channels (upper boundary of the channels) 1 mm below the phantom surface. Adult and neonatal CVMs with 75 µM ctHb levels had a nominal channel diameter of 1.12 mm, yet the mean measured channel diameter -  based on µCT images of a thin multi-channel sample and measuring volume of injected water to ten randomly selected channels - was 0.97 ± 0.04 mm (mean ± standard deviation). The nominal vs. measured channel dimeters for the 46 µM ctHb and 33 µM cases were: 0.90 mm vs. 0.71 mm; 0.80 mm vs. 0.60 mm respectively.  Two 3D-printed lids made of clear resin (120 x 50 x 10 mm) were also attached at the sides of CVM by screws to keep blood and minimize air exposure and subsequent reoxygenation of blood. Blood can be injected into the channels using a 60 ml syringe connected to a manifold in one of 3D-printed side lids to the CVM. 

Implemented - and described more thoroughly - in two published articles:
