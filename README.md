# cerebral-oximetry-phantom
Title: Cerebral oximetry phantom module

Contains STL files for 3D printing of cerebrovascular modules - the main component for a published cerebral oximetry test method

Keywords: Phantom, Cerebral oximeter, NIRS, brain, Blood oxygen saturation

Description:
These cerebrovascular modules (CVM) were developed by Dr. Joshua Pfefer’s group in FDA as part of a novel phantom-based test method for evaluating of cerebral oximetry performance accuracy 

The CVM was fabricated using a stereolithography 3D‐printer (Form2, Formlabs, Inc., Somerville, MA, USA) based on UV-curing of customized methacrylate photopolymer resin composites well‐matched with human brain optical properties.  The CVMs was constructed with overall dimensions of 120 x 120 x 50 mm, with the perfused channel-array region limited to a volume of 120 x 80 x 32.5 mm with channel length of 106 mm in two forms of high and low ctHb levels with modifying the channel diameter and adult – neonate with adjusting customization ratio of resin to represent appropriately optical preparties of the specific age classes.  The CVM incorporated a total of 119 cylindrical linear channels spaced 4.36 mm apart in the vertical and 4.58 – 4.6 mm horizontal directions, with the top row of channels (upper boundary of the channels) 1 mm below the phantom surface. Adult and neonatal CVMs with 75 µM ctHb level had a nominal channel diameter of 1.12 mm, yet the mean measured channel diameter, based on µCT images of a thin multi-channel sample and measuring volume of injected water to ten randomly selected channels was 0.97 ± 0.04 mm (mean ± standard deviation). The nominal vs. measured channel dimeters for the 46 µM ctHb and 33 µM cases were: 0.90 mm vs. 0.71 mm; 0.80 mm vs. 0.60 mm respectively.   Two 3D-printed lids made of clear resin (120 x 50 x 10 mm) were also attached at the sides of CVM by screws to keep blood and minimize air exposure and subsequent reoxygenation of blood. Blood can be injected into the channels using a 60 ml syringe connected to a manifold in one of 3D-printed side lids to the CVM. 

Implemented in two published articles:

(use as primary attribution) Afshari A, Ghassemi P, Halprin M, Lin J, Wang J, Mendoza G, Weininger S, Pfefer TJ. Cerebral oximetry performance testing with a 3D-printed vascular array phantom. Biomed Opt Express, 10(8), 3731-3746, 2019. doi.org/10.1364/BOE.10.003731

Afshari A, Saager R, Burgos D, Vogt W, Wang J, Mendoza G, Weininger S, Sung KB, Durkin AJ, Pfefer TJ. Evaluation of the robustness of cerebral oximetry to variations in skin pigmentation using a novel tissue phantom.  Biomed Opt Express, 13(5):2909-2928, 2022. doi.org/10.1364/BOE.454020
